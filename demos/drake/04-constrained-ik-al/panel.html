The scene shows the arm at every event. After the first overshoot the inner steps move the flange by less than a millimetre and the elbow by less than a degree, so this table, the gauge and the plots carry the changes the scene cannot show.

What one event shows

An accepted joint update is one inner step of the damped Gauss-Newton solve of the augmented objective with the multiplier frozen: the solid arm is the new iterate q, the faded ghost the previous one. A multiplier update moves nothing: the loop holds q, stores the estimate λ + 2μ g (clipped at zero), then starts the next inner solve. The badge at the top names the operation of the selected event and the quantity it kept fixed. The gold sphere is the target, the maroon sphere the flange, the line between them the task error. The protractor is a disc perpendicular to joint 4's axis, fixed to the upper arm: the allowed arc runs from the hardware minimum to the stop, the forbidden arc from the stop to the hardware maximum, and the pointer turns with q4; it is red and larger while the stop is violated by more than the tolerance 10−6 rad. The stop is a software limit chosen for this demonstration: the elbow may not straighten past −2.2 rad (126° of bend). In Deck 05's example (b) the limit kept the two-link elbow from bending further; here it keeps the Panda's elbow from opening.

objectivef(q) = ½ ‖φ(q) − y*‖² [m²]
constraintg(q) = q4 − q4,max ≤ 0 [rad], q4,max = −2.2 rad
augmented objectiveA(q) = f(q) + λ g(q) + μ [g(q) > 0 or λ > 0] g(q)², μ = 1 m²/rad² fixed
inner direction (λ frozen)d = −(J⊤J + 2μ [included] e4e4⊤ + ρ I)−1 ∇A(q), ρ = 10−3
accepted stepΔq = α d, with α = 1 first and halved by Armijo until A(q + αd) ≤ A(q) + 0.01 α ∇A(q)⊤d; the inner solve stops at ‖∇A‖ < 10−7
multiplier update (q fixed)λ ← max(0, λ + 2μ g(q)); stop when max(g,0) < 10−6 rad, ‖∇f + λ∇g‖ < 10−6 m²/rad and |λ g| < 10−8 m² (about the multiplier magnitude times the feasibility tolerance)

Outer iterations are numbered from 0 as in Deck 05 (outer iteration 0 is the squared penalty at μ = 1); the inner steps within each from 1. In run A most inner steps after the first overshoot are shortened by Armijo: that comes from the task term's curvature at a 4.3 cm residual, as in Deck 04's run 3b, not from the stop. The violation after each inner solve falls by the factor a/(a + 2μ) of notes §4.9, about 0.0375 here (a 27-fold reduction), with a the curvature of the best attainable f along the stop.

Run A's answer is a local constrained result reached from the ready pose, not a proof that the target is out of reach. The loop's problem has only the upper bound, and joint angles are periodic: the unconstrained answer with one full turn subtracted from q4 (−8.08 rad) satisfies the bound and reaches the target within 23 nm, outside the hardware range. The comparison program adds the hardware box, which excludes that configuration; SNOPT and IPOPT then agree with the loop's answer from the same start, which supports the local result and is neither an equivalence of the two programs nor a global certificate. Numerical feasibility allows small residuals: IPOPT relaxes bounds by its documented bound_relax_factor.

Run A, Step through outer iteration 0 (14 inner steps): the arm reaches for the far target, the elbow crosses the stop at step 2 (the needle and the pointer turn red, 7.7° past), and the squared penalty pulls it back to 0.32° past the stop while the task error settles near 4.17 cm. Use the slider for the small late steps.
    2. Next multiplier update: nothing moves; λ becomes 2μ times the violation, 0.0112 m²/rad.
    3. Step through outer iteration 1 (6 steps): with the price in force the violation falls about 27 times (the table, the gauge and the violation plot), the task error grows to 4.31 cm, and the next update raises λ to 0.01165.
    4. Slider to the end: λ* = 0.01167 m²/rad, the elbow on the stop within the tolerance, 4.32 cm short. Tick the unconstrained answer: the red stick figure reaches the target with the elbow 0.41 rad past the stop. Say that this is a local result from the ready pose: one full turn of the elbow would satisfy the bound and reach the target, outside the hardware range. Relaxing the stop by 1° would lower f* by about λ* × 0.0175, which SNOPT confirms.
    5. Run B (the plan's target): the elbow stays 0.03 rad inside the stop, the row is never included, λ stays 0, and the loop is plain damped Gauss-Newton (Deck 05's example (e)).
