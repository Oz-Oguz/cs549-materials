Readouts

The columns of R are drawn: x_B, y_B, z_B are the red, green and blue arrows, and the numbers in each column are that arrow's world coordinates. Active: the book turns and its corner p goes somewhere new. Passive: nothing moves but the frame, and the same corner gets new coordinates. One matrix, two questions — say which one you are asking.

Default state: Rz(30°), the tray of the worked example (notes §5). Companion notes: notes.md.

Readouts

Left book: a then b, which is the product RbRa. The rotation applied first is written last because it acts on the vector first. Right book: b then a. Both start aligned with the world. After one step they have received different first turns. After two steps they have received the same two turns in opposite orders. The readouts always describe the books currently shown.

Default state: a = 90° about x, b = 90° about z, both already applied: the first columns are (0, 1, 0) and (0, 0, 1) — notes §4.5.

Readouts

The three rings represent the gimbals: the blue ring turns about world z (yaw), the green ring about the yaw-turned y axis (pitch), and the red ring about the yaw-and-pitch-turned x axis (roll). At pitch +90° or −90°, the yaw and roll axes share one line. Their effects have opposite signs at +90° and the same sign at −90°. The angle coordinates lose one independent instantaneous rotation direction. A freely rotating body still has three rotational freedoms. Drag the pitch away from either lock and the axes separate.

Default state: ψ = 10°, ϑp = 90°, ϕ = 0° — locked; notes §4.7 and §5 (g).

Four spellings of the same orientation: the matrix from Rodrigues' formula, the axis–angle pair, the unit quaternion r = (w, x, y, z) = (cos θ/2, sin θ/2 û), and the two RAI .g spellings.
