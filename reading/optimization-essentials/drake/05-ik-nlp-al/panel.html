What changed, and what one event shows

What changed against the earlier demos. There the squared target error was the objective and the distance a displayed diagnostic. Here the target is a rail: h(q) = φ(q) − y* = 0 must hold at a successful answer within tolerance, and the distance ‖h‖ measures its violation. The objective is the posture deviation ½‖q − qref‖². The joint limits are fence rows that must also hold at the answer. Intermediate iterates may violate the rail and the fences, and neither the posture cost nor a violation need change monotonically through the solve.

An accepted joint update is one inner step of the damped Gauss-Newton solve of the augmented objective with κ and λ frozen: the solid arm is the new iterate, the faded ghost the previous one. A multiplier update moves nothing: the loop holds q and stores κ̂ = κ + 2μh h and max(0, λ + 2μg g). The badge names the operation of the selected event and the quantity it kept fixed.

The rail rows are in metres and the fence rows in radians, so the two penalty weights are separate and the two feasibility residuals are reported separately. The weights were measured (μh = 1 does not converge in 30 outer iterations; 1000/100 converges in 3 and 5). The complementarity threshold 10−8 rad² is a fixed value; in run B stationarity and complementarity both fail at outer iteration 3 and both pass at iteration 4. An inner solve that ends at the precision floor is labelled so, not "stationary". κ is the price of moving the rail: a 1 mm shift of the target in x changes f* by about −κx × 0.001 (checked by re-solving on the same branch). At convergence, moving the target along the arrow −κ̂ raises f* fastest, at about ‖κ*‖ rad² per metre (2.3 in run A, 7.7 in run B); before convergence the arrow shows only the loop's current estimate, clamped at 0.30 m while ‖κ̂‖ exceeds 15 rad²/m. Outer iterations are numbered from 0 as in Deck 05; the inner steps within each from 1.

Local results. The loop finds a KKT point from its start. Run B's target lies on the robot's symmetry plane. From the exactly symmetric ready pose, SNOPT stops at a KKT point that is not a minimum (cost 1.729, λ = 4.196): turning joints 1, 3 and 5 together, with the flange kept on the target and joint 2 on its limit, lowers the cost (curvature −1.40); such a point is called a constrained saddle. The loop leaves that point, but rounding would decide which mirror image it reaches, so run B starts with joint 1 at +0.01 rad, which makes the run repeatable; the whole run decides the side (the first steps cross the plane), and a larger offset can reach the mirror answer. The answer (cost 1.499, λ = 1.053) and its mirror image have the same cost. Solver answers are compared on cost, feasibility, stationarity and complementarity; multipliers and prices on the same branch; nothing here claims a global minimum.

    Suggested sequence

    1. Run A, paused at the ready pose: the posture cost is zero and the flange is 22 cm off the rail. Ask what the loop will do from here now that the target is a rail and the posture is the cost.
    2. Step through outer iteration 0: the penalty pulls the flange toward the rail while the posture cost rises; the penalty alone stops 1.14 mm short; the next update stores κ = 2μh h exactly, and slide 24's reading of the shortfall as ‖κ*‖/(2μh) (1.15 mm) is approximate on this nonlinear arm. Next multiplier update: nothing moves, κ becomes 2μh h and the KKT row appears with the rail mark still ✗; tick the arrow if useful (−κ̂ at the flange, an estimate).
    3. Step through outer iteration 1: its first step cuts the violation from 1.1 mm to about 9 µm, still above the 1e-6 m tolerance, so the rail mark stays ✗ at the next update; outer iteration 2 brings it below the tolerance and the third update turns the mark ✓. Slider to the end: on the rail within tolerance, κ* beside slide 19's column, f* = 0.209 beside its cost.
    4. Run B: the start is the ready pose with joint 1 turned by 0.01 rad, so the posture cost begins small and positive (5e-5 rad²). Joint 2 reaches its lower limit; a fence row joins the rail with its own λ (1.053). Say the local-result sentence: the symmetric answer is a saddle, the mirror minimum has the same cost.
